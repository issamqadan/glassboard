How Glassboard works

Not how to play chess — Learn does that. This is how to use the glass-box assistance that makes Glassboard different: transparent, adjustable, and shared by both sides.

🔍 The Glass Lens

Your in-game helper. It shows one thing that matters right now — and it lives on the board, so you never scroll to find help.

▶
Play

Plays the suggested move. The same move is also a glowing arrow on the board — tap that to play it too.

💡
Explain

Toggles plain-language explanations — "Wins their knight", "Defends your bishop", "Puts the king in check". On by default; tap to hide.

👁
Show

Previews the move on the board without playing it.

⋯
Other moves

A short list of alternatives, each with what it does.

🧭
Plans

Opens the strategy picker for this position (see Strategies below).

📜
Log

The glass-box record — every bit of help either side took.

🔔 How help arrives

Set when you start a game. It controls when the move-answer appears — safety highlights and awareness are always on.

☀️
Open Hand

Help is always on screen. Play freely — the friendliest for learning.

🔔
On Call

Help is hidden until you summon it. Every ask is counted, so you can measure how independent you're becoming.

🤝
Gentleman's Game

Start with none. Request help mid-game — your opponent must allow it. (Great human-vs-human.)

🎚️ How much help

🚫
No help

Pure chess — just you and the board.

♾️
Full help

Threats, plans, and the single best move, explained. Learn as you play. recommended for beginners

🎚️
Choose level

Pick exactly how much — from safety hints up to a co-pilot that can play the plan for you.

🤖 The AI plays glass too

Assistance isn't a one-sided crutch — it's a layer both sides share, in the open.

⛏
AI matching your help

The more you lean on the suggestions, the more the AI digs deeper toward full strength — so the game stays a fair fight. You'll see a badge when it does, with the exact share of turns you took help.

🛟
Lifelines

Under pressure, the AI can spend a lifeline to find a stronger move — and the move is marked on the board. "You needed help there too."

📜 The glass-box record

Nothing is hidden. Every move you play is tagged by how you found it, and both players can see it.

💪
Found it on your own

You played it without taking the suggestion.

🤝
Took help

You played the suggested move, or asked to see it. It's on the record — that's the point.

📖 Strategies

Glassboard names what's being played and can guide a plan — for you and your opponent.

🎯
Identity strip

Top of the Lens: names your opening/plan and the opponent's, plus the game phase.

🧭
Plans picker

Adopt a plan that fits the position — the engine's live plans, grouped by type.

📖
Follow the book

In the opening, follow your chosen line — but only when it's also the engine's best move, so it's never a bad "book" move.

✨
Strategies library

Browse every named opening + middlegame/endgame plan, by phase, family, and complexity — with a demo. Open it →

🏠 Ways to play

🤖
Play the AI

A solo game against an opponent you size to your level. Saved automatically — resume any time, on any device.

＋
New game — invite a friend

Create a game and share the link. Match sets a fair handicap for whoever's rated lower; Casual gives both players unlimited help, no ratings.

⏱
Clocks, undo, rematch

Optional clock, take back a move, and a rematch that keeps your settings.

📈 Getting better

💪
Independence score

At game end, see how many moves you found on your own, and whether that's trending up. The goal is to need less help over time.

🧠
Player model

Glassboard learns where you tend to slip (hung pieces, missed free material) and leans your help toward what you actually need.
