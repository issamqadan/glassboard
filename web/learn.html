Chess where the help is in the open

Normally, a strong player and a beginner can’t have a fun game — it’s over too fast. Glassboard fixes that with a declared handicap: the weaker player gets AI assistance, and the wider the gap in skill, the more help they get.

The one rule that makes it fair: every bit of help is shown to your opponent, too. Nothing is hidden. It’s not cheating — it’s an agreed, visible head-start, like strokes in golf.

The idea in three steps
  • Set your levels. Each player says how well they play (a tap is enough).
  • Glassboard sizes the help. It gives the weaker side assistance matched to the gap.
  • Play in the open. Both players see every hint — and as the weaker player improves, the help fades away.

The goal of chess

Two armies face off on an 8×8 board. Your aim is to trap the other player’s King so it can’t escape being captured — that’s checkmate, and it wins the game. You don’t need to memorize anything to start; Glassboard will help you.

Below: each piece and how it moves. The blue dots show where a piece in the middle could go.

A few more things
  • Check: when a King is under attack. You must get it to safety immediately.
  • Checkmate: a King in check with no way out — game over.
  • Promotion: get a pawn to the far end and it turns into a Queen.
  • Taking turns: White moves first, then players alternate.
